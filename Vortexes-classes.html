vortices!!!

what even are vortices? Well they are a circular pattern of air the stems from behind a aircrafts wing (for helicopters its there spinning blades) and different factors can change the power and factors of vortices. There are three specific factors that produces a strong vortices.

  1. wight of aircraft
  2. shape of wing
  3. speed of aircraft

Let's go down the list and explain each one. First, for weight, the heavier the aircraft is, the bigger the vortex is going to be. Why is this? Well, it's because the bigger aircraft need more power to lift off the ground. Next, when talking about the wing shape, we also need to take into account the wing size; the bigger the wing size, the more surface area air will pass over the wing, which will cause less vortex (higher wing surface area = less vortices; less surface area = more vortex power). So, when the flaps are down on a plane, the vortexes are bigger than if all the flaps were up. This is because when all the flaps are down, the air goes over the wing and passes over with more air per square inch, whereas if the flaps were up, there would be less air going up and over the wings and less air hitting the wing per square inch. Finally, speed is a determining factor in the strength of the vortices: the faster an aircraft is going, the weaker its vortices are going to be, but the slower the plane is going, the bigger and stronger the vortex is going to be. This is because at high speeds there is less of a chance for a vortex to form because the aircraft is cutting through the air very fast, but when an aircraft is going slower, there is more of a chance a big vortex is going to happen!!!!
